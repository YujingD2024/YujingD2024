## Open Position

Ph.D. Position–Fluid and Fracture Dynamics

The Ph.D. position offers research opportunities across the laboratory’s research areas, with projects tailored to the student’s background and interests. Funding is available through Research Assistantships (RA) and Teaching Assistantships (TA).

## Responsibilities

Research activities will vary by project and may include:

Ph.D. Position:

1. Design and conduct experiments in natural and engineered porous and fractured materials and custom-fabricated analog models.
2. Develop and operate experimental systems to investigate fluid flow, transport, fracture propagation, and material response.
3. Perform high-speed imaging, microscopy, acoustic emission monitoring, and pressure and flow measurements to characterize dynamic processes.
4. Characterize the rheological and interfacial properties of complex fluids and the mechanical and acoustic responses of materials.
5. Analyze experimental data, identify governing physical mechanisms, and develop quantitative relationships and predictive models.

Both students are expected to participate in research discussions, maintain accurate experimental records, present research findings, and contribute to scientific publications.

## Career opportunities

Students will receive interdisciplinary research training, hands-on experience with advanced experimental techniques, and professional development. The training and research experience can support various academic and industry career pathways, including:

1. Academic and research careers at universities and national laboratories.
2. Opportunities for summer internships and research collaborations in academic, national laboratory, and industry settings.
3. Technical and research careers in the oil and gas, geothermal, energy, and advanced materials industries.

## Qualifications

We welcome motivated applicants from diverse engineering and science backgrounds with strong analytical abilities, an interest in experimental research, and a commitment to scientific discovery.

Basic Qualifications:

1. B.S. and/or M.S. degree in engineering, physics, or other related science fields, with a minimum GPA of 3.0/4.0 or equivalent.
2. English proficiency meeting the University of Tulsa’s graduate admission requirements (published minimums include TOEFL iBT 80 on the original 0–120 scale or IELTS 6.5, with applicable exemptions). GRE requirements and potential waivers are subject to university and program policies.
3. Strong analytical, written, and oral communication skills, with the ability to work independently and collaboratively.
4. Self-motivation, research curiosity, and a strong commitment to research and problem-solving.

Preferred Qualifications (Not Required):

Applicants with experience in one or more of the following areas are particularly encouraged to apply:

1. Experimental fluid mechanics, fracture mechanics, materials science, or porous-media transport.
2. Microfabrication, microfluidics, microscopy, or 3D printing.
3. Material characterization, mechanical testing, or microstructural analysis.
4. High-speed imaging, particle image velocimetry (PIV), particle tracking, or image processing.
5. Computational fluid dynamics (CFD), discrete element modeling (DEM), or coupled CFD–DEM simulations.
6. Finite element modeling (FEM), fracture simulation, or coupled hydro-mechanical modeling.
7. Scientific programming, numerical analysis, or data processing using Python, MATLAB, or similar tools.
